Kaggle's dataset metadata lists ODC Attribution (ODC-By) for both downloads. The raw files are kept
locally under the ignored `data/raw/` directory and must not be committed or redistributed without
reviewing the dataset pages and license obligations. The raw files and generated feed are ignored by
Git. The generated feed powers the local simulator when the Kaggle feed option is enabled. No
instructor-supplied GigSim, persona corpora, gold dev or hidden test set, or final policy update is
present; the local simulator and its outcomes are our own implementation and are not the instructor
service.

## Preprocessing and fields

The job-postings CSV has 53,058 rows and these fields: `title`, `link`, `description`,
`published_date`, `is_hourly`, `hourly_low`, `hourly_high`, `budget`, and `country`. The `link` field
is used only for local duplicate profiling and should not be exposed in generated public fixtures.
There were no duplicate non-empty links in the local file. The hourly bounds and fixed budget are
missing for roughly 57-59% of records; the data also has a long-tailed fixed-budget field, so extreme
values need validation before use. The records are historical scraped postings, not a live feed.

The contracts CSV has 1,292,791 rows and fields `job_id`, `job_title`, `start_date`, `end_date`,
`freelancer_id`, `total_hours`, `total_paid`, and `hourly_rate`. `end_date` is missing for 30.7% and
`hourly_rate` for 61.6%. `total_hours` is zero for at least half the rows, so naïve division of
`total_paid` by hours is unsafe. Identifiers should be treated as pseudonymous and excluded from
public outputs. These records are observational dataset records, not verified outcomes for the
postings in the other Kaggle dataset.

Run `python -m evals.runners.prepare_kaggle_data` to make the ignored `data/processed/gigsim_seed.json`.
The default deterministic SHA-256 ordering takes at most 200 distinct non-empty postings; it is a
capped convenience sample, not a random or representative sample. The original posting URL is used
only as an input to a one-way stable ID and is not copied into the seed. Email addresses, URLs, and
phone-like strings in titles/descriptions are replaced with placeholders. Text is whitespace
normalized and truncated to 8,000 characters. Skills are extracted only by exact phrase matching
against the script's documented allowlist; unrecognized skills are omitted, not inferred. Hourly
budgets use available nonnegative hourly bounds capped at USD 100,000; otherwise a positive fixed
budget up to USD 1,000,000 is retained. Missing/invalid budgets are explicitly marked unspecified.
The output also includes 40 authored unpaid/off-platform and prompt-injection test scenarios,
clearly marked `synthetic_scenario`; their base posting is copied into each scenario. They are
adversarial test fixtures, not Kaggle labels.

The contracts CSV is summarized only: rows with positive hourly rates up to USD 1,000 yield a
median of USD 21.00; positive total-paid rows yield a median of USD 191.67. Invalid, missing, and
zero hourly rates are excluded from those summaries. The contract records are not joined to
postings and are not used as client outcomes or pricing recommendations. The pricing node uses
synthetic profile floors constrained by each posting's stated budget; it does not claim that the
unmatched contract aggregates are a job-specific market rate.

All client IDs, histories, hidden preferences, budgets ceilings, Connects balances, and simulated
reply/hire outcomes in GigSim are synthetic. Client preferences are generated deterministically
from the stable job ID. The simulator makes no assertion about the actual client associated with a
Kaggle posting. In the runtime, the starter-kit jobs remain available; set
`GIGBID_USE_KAGGLE_FEED=true` to include the generated local seed. The portfolio retriever uses the
selected synthetic persona's authored evidence; its retrieval variants remain lexical and do not
perform PDF parsing, GitHub ingestion, neural embedding, vector database indexing, or incremental
uploads.

The dev labels are authored transparently: the only positive job maps to `evidence-001`; the unpaid
off-platform listing is labeled skip/risk; no evidence is labeled relevant for that scam example.
These are smoke-test expectations, not independent gold judgments. Neither Kaggle source provides
GigBid's per-persona relevance, bid/skip, scam, fair-price, proposal-quality, or simulated client
outcome labels.

## Limitations and safeguards

The Kaggle job collection is historical and scraped; the contract collection has substantial
missingness and questionable zero-hour records. Neither has been independently audited against the
live platform or linked to the other. They cannot establish actual GigBid client reply/hire outcomes
or replace hidden evaluation. The local simulator's deterministic outcomes are useful for repeatable
software tests only and must not be reported as observed marketplace performance. The synthetic
persona examples must be replaced with evidence the user owns or is authorized to use before any
real-world proposal use. Logs/audit records avoid proposal body text and redact PII before any
future trace export. Never commit hidden evaluation data or real client records.
